## Why Open Source?

Open source offers a chance to ‘decentre’ the underpinnings digital education (Macgilchrist et al., 2024, p. 15). It creates opportunity for global oversight of and contribution to the code that describes the infrastructures and intermediaries of a digital future. The European Commission (2020, p. 5) is a supporter for that and many other reasons:

> _‘open source is independent of companies and countries. It minimises the risk of vendor lock-in and getting caught up in political shenanigans or trade disputes’_

Bayne’s (2024, p. 517) application of architecture thinking to digital education attends to ‘material infrastructures, models of knowledge production and geographies’ as scholarly implications of the political power inherent in imagining outside the box. Open source too often sits outwith the digital education box.

While open source is an opportunity, it is not a solution by default. Macgilchrist (2024, p. 19) notes that all technical solutions carry baggage, including open source. Technology is invariably built upon existing code, pulling forward approaches and constraints that bias designers of the now towards avenues of development determined in the past.

There are other tensions to consider, for example the ‘Stay in control’ interoperability imperative might involve political decision-making about which of many standards to adopt (European Commission, 2020, p. 10). As we grapple with shifting and interconnected problems, the critical application of futuring methods such as ‘Utopia as method’ can mediate the move towards open source futures, and expand horizons beyond the closure of proprietary platforms (Facer, 2016, pp. 74–75). You will attempt exactly that now.

## Collaboration

The imagination and perspectives required to re-orient digital education are many. One person constructing a ‘good society’ will find it difficult to imagine the experiences of others regardless of empathetic capacity – we are all constrained by our own subjectivities (Romero-Hall et al., 2026, p. 289). In contrast, co-construction may lead to dynamic discussions and experiences that tease out diverse collective learnings. Within the limited confines of this OER it is difficult to apply, but please try to engage with this process collaboratively in the future. A starting point could be commenting on each other’s activity results in the Padlet!
