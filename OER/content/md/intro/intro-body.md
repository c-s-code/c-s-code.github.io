## Hidden Curriculum

The hidden curriculum in education refers to the implicit learnings embedded within and transmitted alongside instruction (Gallagher, Breines and Blaney, 2021, p. 437). Hidden curricula have been widely critiqued as reproducing inequity by reinforcing existing socio-cultural norms, such as pushing working-class students into vocational jobs over further education (Edwards, 2015, pp. 267–268).

However, Giroux and Penna (1979, p. 32) also remark that the hidden curriculum has the capacity to be vehicle for positive change through critical analysis and challenging existing structures and assumptions.

As education becomes technologically embedded, digital actors impart their own hidden curricula ‘in new modalities of knowing, perceiving, and acting’ (Adams, 2017, p. 238). Taking a critical view of digital education, you will note that through technology an increasing number of external actors are gaining the influence and power to embed their own hidden curricula.

## Resilience

This OER will look to tackle a neglected hidden curriculum of resilience – one that empowers commercial interests, undermines digital sovereignty, and shifts responsibility onto individuals – through open source interventions.

You will apply Sian Bayne’s interpretation of Levitas’s three modes to interrogate resilience discourse in digital education and how that seeks to shape the future, before considering how you might employ open source initiatives to architect preferred outcomes.
